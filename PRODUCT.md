# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The public site serves four groups whose needs overlap but are not identical:

- Prospective members, especially Indonesian students who want a low-pressure place to practise English and meet peers.
- Current members looking for upcoming activities, recent stories, and a shared record of the club.
- Campus partners, speakers, and community organisations checking the club's credibility before collaborating.
- Alumni and supporters who want a quick view of the club's work and a clear contact path.

Most visits will happen on phones between classes or after an event link is shared in chat. A visitor should understand what the club does, try one small language interaction, see selective proof of real activity, and find the next useful action without reading a long institutional profile.

## Product Purpose

Create a public home for the working-name **English Club** that combines a concise organisation profile, a focused membership landing page, a consent-gated member directory, and an editorial blog. The site should let visitors experience a small, safe act of conversational English, understand how responsibility is organised, then use the photo archive as selective evidence of an active community. Member profiles, events, stories, and contact information remain maintainable through Convex.

Success means a new visitor can answer these questions quickly: Who is this club for? What happens inside it? Does it feel welcoming? How can I join or make contact? A partner should also find enough context to take the club seriously.

The repository does not contain a confirmed legal name, address, membership price, member count, or verified production member roster. The current logo, atmospheric people imagery, and Member portrait sheet are generated working assets. The announced development deployment contains 15 explicitly fictional profiles and five managed divisions so the complete organisation, filtering, and consent contracts can be tested through Convex. These records are development data, do not establish the club's real roster or member total, and must be removed or replaced by consent-cleared records before production.

## Brand Personality

**Candid, curious, and assured.**

The voice sounds like capable students speaking to other students: warm without sales language, intelligent without becoming academic, and playful without turning the club into a youth-brand template. Real conversation, shared work, and cultural exchange matter more than polished claims.

The intended emotional sequence is recognition first, then curiosity, then enough confidence to join or contact the club.

## Anti-references

- Generic education sites built from equal icon cards, stock graduation photos, and inflated outcome claims.
- Luxury-club styling that borrows prestige cues but hides the members and everyday activity.
- Youth-organisation pages that stack every fact, committee member, metric, gallery image, and form on one oversized homepage.
- Editorial affectations unrelated to the club: repeated tiny labels, numbered sections, fake archive metadata, decorative rules, and forced serif italics.
- Blue corporate templates that could belong to any campus organisation.
- Fabricated testimonials, member counts, awards, dates, or programme details.

## Design Principles

1. **Words are the instrument.** Lead with a complete sentence and a bounded action that demonstrates conversation.
2. **Photographs are receipts.** Use documentary media where it supports a sourced point; never make the archive the layout engine.
3. **Practice feels social.** Show English as something members do together in rooms, conversations, exchanges, and community work.
4. **One decision per fold.** Each section should answer one visitor question and offer at most one relevant next action.
5. **Editorial, not institutional.** Blog and event material should read with a calm publication rhythm, while organisation details stay concise and scannable.
6. **Facts earn permanence.** Confirmed content lives in Convex; uncertain material remains marked as draft data and never appears as an official claim.
7. **Profiles require permission.** A public name and role need cleared profile consent; a portrait needs its own cleared consent.
8. **Development data stays development data.** Fictional profiles may exercise the real Convex directory on the named development deployment, but they never conceal an unavailable backend, enter production, or substitute for consent-cleared organisation records.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across colour contrast, keyboard operation, visible focus, semantic headings, form labels, error handling, and touch targets. Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; content cannot depend on animation. Photos need useful alt text that describes the visible action without identifying people who are not named in source material.

The interface must work at 320 px width, tolerate text enlargement, avoid colour-only meaning, and keep English copy plain enough for visitors who are still learning the language. Indonesian supporting text may clarify high-stakes actions such as joining and contact.
